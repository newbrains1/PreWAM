Abstract

World-action models combine action generation with future prediction, allowing visual transitions to serve as supervision for policy learning. Our controlled study of prediction spaces shows that spatially structured semantic features support better overall generalization under visual perturbations and make motion information more readily recoverable from learned predictive representations.

Building on these observations, we introduce PreWAM, a world-action model that organizes future supervision around state changes in a prediction space defined by pretrained DINO features. Specifically, Residual World Prediction jointly learns source-relative changes and their resulting future states to emphasize dynamic information while preserving future-state semantics. Temporal-Scale Prediction conditions world queries on temporal distance to capture both short-term local motion and longer-horizon state evolution.

These predictive objectives jointly shape a shared vision-language backbone with action supervision during pretraining and downstream policy learning. Experiments across the LIBERO benchmark family, SimplerEnv, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world robotic manipulation demonstrate strong manipulation performance and improved generalization and robustness under diverse distribution shifts.

Contributions

  1. Understanding prediction spaces. Controlled comparisons show that similar performance on standard tasks can conceal substantial differences in generalization. Spatial semantic supervision provides the strongest overall robustness to visual perturbations.
  2. Learning state changes across temporal scales. PreWAM combines Residual World Prediction and Temporal-Scale Prediction to learn source-relative changes over multiple horizons, jointly shaping the shared representations used for action generation.
  3. Generalization across tasks and embodiments. Experiments demonstrate strong performance across the LIBERO benchmark family, SimplerEnv, and RoboTwin 2.0, complemented by single-arm and bimanual manipulation on a physical Piper robot.

CONTROLLED STUDY

Which prediction space supports policy learning?

We study how future prediction targets shape policy generalization by comparing DINOv3 spatial semantic features, Wan 2.1 VAE reconstruction latents, and V-JEPA 2 predictive video features in a shared policy framework.

Generalization under visual changes

The three policies differ by just 0.20 percentage points on LIBERO, but separate more clearly under LIBERO-Plus perturbations. DINOv3 provides the strongest overall generalization, with clear advantages over reconstruction supervision under camera, background, and lighting changes.

Motion readout and visual attention

World queries encode recoverable end-effector motion. DINOv3 supervision gives the lowest displacement-readout error under camera shifts, noise, and background changes, while action-query attention highlights manipulation-relevant regions.

PREWAM

PreWAM: Unifying Action Generation and Latent World Prediction

PreWAM integrates action generation and latent world prediction through a shared vision-language backbone. Given current observations and a language instruction, the backbone produces action queries for the action expert and world queries for the world predictor. Joint action and predictive supervision shapes representations that support both robot control and anticipation of visual state transitions.

Building on our prediction-space study, PreWAM predicts future states in a frozen DINOv3 feature space. Residual World Prediction models changes relative to a source state, while Temporal-Scale Prediction organizes supervision across short, medium, and long horizons.

Residual World Prediction

Starting from a source visual state, the predictor estimates a feature change. Adding this residual reconstructs a future state.

Temporal-Scale Prediction

Horizon-conditioned world queries learn short-, medium-, and long-range transitions within a sampled clip.

Training and Inference

Action learning and world prediction jointly train the shared backbone, with supervision determined by the data source.

Pretraining

Starting from a pretrained VLM, PreWAM jointly learns the backbone, world predictor, and action expert. Training alternates DROID robot demonstrations with Something-Something V2 human videos. Robot demonstrations provide both action and visual-transition supervision; human videos provide visual-transition supervision through the world-modeling objective.

Posttraining

The downstream policy inherits the backbone, world predictor, action expert, and learned queries from pretraining. It adapts to the target manipulation setting using demonstrations with joint action and world-modeling objectives. The target encoder remains frozen throughout pretraining and posttraining.

Inference

The VLM processes current observations and the language instruction. Conditioned on the resulting action-query representations and available proprioception, the action expert generates an action chunk through flow matching. World queries remain in the backbone input, preserving the training-time token context. The target encoder and world predictor are not executed.

ABLATIONS AND ANALYSIS

Effects of predictive supervision and pretraining

RWP improves LIBERO-Plus success from 76.6% to 77.7%. Adding TSP further raises it to 79.1% and improves LIBERO-Long from 95.0% to 97.0%. World modeling and pretraining also contribute to long-horizon performance and generalization.

Action stability under visual perturbations

PreWAM produces more stable actions under brightness, contrast, color-shift, and image-compression perturbations. At 40% brightness and contrast reduction, action-MSE increases are 44.8% and 52.9% lower than without world modeling.
